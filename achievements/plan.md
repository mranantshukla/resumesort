Owner: Anant Shukla

## Purpose

Achievements are quick credibility points. Interviewers use them to check consistency and to start conversation. Each achievement should have a one-line summary, a 30-second story, and one fact you can defend.

## JD alignment ([JD.md](../JD.md))

Achievements are supporting evidence, not the main story. Use them for:
- **JD #4 (structured thinking on ambiguous problems)**: Policy Challenge '24 1st rank, BYOP 2026 Top 3 (Consulting and Analytics).
- **JD #7 (communicating with many teams)**: Udaan 5.0 guest speaker, OMI Hack-a-thon panel presentation.
- **JD #1 (curiosity)**: Heritage Excellence Award (breadth of interests).

Keep total prep here to about 2 hours.

## Resume checks

1. **Missing years.** Achievement 4 (Udaan 5.0) and achievement 5 (Chess) have no year. Add them to the resume or be ready to say when.
2. **Achievement 2 spacing.** "1st Rank" is listed under the same line as "Policy Challenge '24" but the placement looks like a sub-bullet. Confirm the exact name of the event and the organizer.
3. **Consistent CGPA.** The resume says CGPA 7.405 in a table labeled "CGPA/Percentage." Make sure the label and the scale (10-point) are clear.

## Story prep (30 seconds each)

Use this format: "[Event], [what I did], [what happened], [what I learned]."

1. **Bring Your Own Projects 2026 (top 3)**: Start with the project problem, your method, and the criterion that set you apart. Keep the metric you used to measure the project.
2. **Policy Challenge '24 (1st rank)**: Lead with the problem statement, the data or evidence you used, and the solution's trade-off. Judges reward structured thinking.
3. **OMI Hack-a-thon panel**: The presentation skill and handling critique. Mention one panel question you could not answer at first and what you did next.
4. **Heritage Excellence Award**: Show breadth. Link it to the POR and project work in one sentence.
5. **Udaan 5.0 guest speaker**: The communication story. Shows you can explain complex ideas to a large audience. Mention the number of students as a fact.
6. **Chess 1533 (Rapid)**: Use it for the "thinking under time pressure" and "pattern recognition" story. Keep it to one sentence unless asked for more. Do not overclaim.

## Likely questions and suggested answers (outline only)

- "Tell me about the competition you placed top 3 in." Answer: problem, your method, the one metric that proved it worked, and what you would improve.
- "What did you learn from winning a policy hackathon?" Answer: how to scope a problem in a short time and present it with a clear recommendation and trade-offs.
- "How do you handle a tough audience?" Answer: use the panel or student-audience story. Describe how you adapted the message.
- "What do you do when you're stuck on a problem?" Answer: use the chess story. Break the problem into candidate moves, evaluate each, and pick a path with a time limit.
