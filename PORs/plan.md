## Purpose

PORs are your proof for two JD lines that projects alone cannot prove:
- **JD #7**: collaborating with Product, Business, Engineering, Design and Operations.
- **JD #5**: high ownership from problem identification to execution and impact measurement.

In a Product Analyst loop, PORs come up in the HR / hiring-manager round and as follow-ups ("you managed a budget; how did you decide where money went?"). The trap is telling them as event-management stories. Tell them as **operations analytics** stories: a system, a metric, a decision.

## POR 1: Overall Coordinator, Hostel Affairs Council (January 2026 to Present)

### Resume claims
- Led and coordinated hostel operations for 8,000+ residents across 18+ hostels.
- Oversaw budgeting, resource allocation and vendor coordination for 10+ large-scale institute-wide events.
- Developed tech platforms, streamlining hostel management processes and enhancing student engagement.

### Questions to prepare
- What did "efficient administration" mean in numbers? (Complaint resolution time, mess satisfaction, cost per resident, rebate processing time.)
- How did you prioritize when 18 hostels wanted different things? (Impact x urgency; a simple scoring sheet.)
- Vendor coordination: how did you compare vendors? (Price, quality incidents, on-time delivery; a scorecard.)
- "Enhancing student engagement": what metric? (Appetizer active users, event attendance, feedback response rate.)
- A conflict with a warden, vendor or student body and how you resolved it.

### Analyst exercise
Build a one-page "hostel ops dashboard" design: KPIs (complaints open, median resolution days, meals served vs expected, rebate claims), trend, breakdown by hostel, and one alert rule. This links the POR to JD #6 directly.

## POR 2: Overall Finance Coordinator, Unnat Bharat Abhiyan (January 2024 to Present)

### Resume claims
- Managed a Rs 5+ lakh annual CSR budget for sustainable development across 5 villages impacting 2,700+ residents.
- Directed financial operations for 200+ team members, overseeing budgeting for projects benefitting 800+ students.
- Strengthened public relations and outreach with government officials, NGOs and industry stakeholders.

### Questions to prepare
- How was the budget split across villages and projects? What was the logic? (Need, reach, cost per beneficiary, sustainability.)
- How was "impacting 2,700+ residents" counted? Unique people or total touchpoints? Avoid double counting.
- A time you cut or denied a budget request. How did you explain it?
- How did you track spend vs plan? Any variance and what you did.
- What do you report to a CSR sponsor? (Outputs vs outcomes; this is the same skill as reporting feature impact.)

### Analyst exercise
Build an Excel sheet: projects as rows; cost, beneficiaries, cost per beneficiary, expected outcome, priority score. Show how a 20% budget cut would change allocation. Good evidence for Excel skill (JD #8) and trade-off thinking (JD #4).

## POR 3: Joint Secretary, Social Empowerment Through Upliftment, NSS IIT Roorkee (June 2025 to Present)

### Resume claims
- Mobilized 1,000+ volunteers for plantation drives and medicine collection initiatives.
- Directed 17 cross-functional teams for Flood Relief, Blood Donation and Road Safety Month.
- Fostered partnerships for socio-innovation, women empowerment and rural development goals.

### Questions to prepare
- How did you get 1,000+ volunteers? Funnel: reached, signed up, showed up. Which channel converted best? (This is literally a funnel analysis; say so.)
- How did you coordinate 17 teams? Owners, check-ins, a tracker.
- One drive that went badly and what changed the next time.
- Outcome metric per drive: units of blood collected, trees planted and surviving, kits distributed.

### Analyst exercise
Write the volunteer funnel with real or estimated numbers and one experiment you would run to improve show-up rate (e.g., reminder message A vs B).

- Tell me about a time you led without authority.
- Tell me about a conflict with a stakeholder.
- Tell me about a time you had to say no.
- How do you manage multiple priorities?
- Tell me about a failure.
